# How it works

For people changing the code. Tests and the GitHub checks are in [`DEVELOPMENT.md`](../DEVELOPMENT.md).

## Docker logging

The servers log DEBUG and INFO lines to stdout and WARN and ERROR lines to stderr (`lib/logs.py`), so Docker, and its journald driver, can tell them apart; under systemd, where both go to the journal, each line starts with its syslog priority (`<4>` ...), which the journal takes as its level and strips, so `journalctl` colours warnings and errors. `compose.yaml` gives the container Docker's `local` log driver, which compresses and rotates its files (5 files of 10 MB). Read the log with `docker logs -tf ecobot-mcp` (`-t` for the timestamps Docker records).

The sources (ecobot-mcp's) have the same shape: `start()`, `warm()`, `close()`, and a `warmer` that refreshes them on a timer. ecobot-mcp's start (`ecobot_mcp/core.py`) runs each `start()` without waiting on any server (`start(wait=False)`: it is more important to be listening and answering than to have the newest readings), then opens the port while each `warm()` runs once in the background (`Core.warm_up`), and the clients are told when the fresh readings come in (the MCP resources' `ResourceUpdated`, the dashboard's `/dashboard/events`). Each source starts from what the data folder kept: the station's device (its address, name, time zone, place and age, as its device list last gave them; else the station the file's readings are of, in the file's time zone) and its last live reading, the air sensor's last reading (both kept in the file at most once a minute), the last forecast and pollen page, Home Assistant's first state of the station (looked for once, a long history query, after the start). Until a device's first reading of this run comes in, `current` and the dashboard answer from the kept one, as of its time (the request for it made meanwhile), and the alerts of a device start only once it has been read. The station's device list is asked for in the background (the cloud's and Home Assistant's together), and again after a failure, sooner at first then every 5 minutes, until it answers: a station found in the file is used meanwhile, and a new address or time zone it names is taken at the next start. On a first start, with nothing kept, the station's time zone is the configured one (`TZ`) for that run, its readings "not in yet" until the device list answers; the forecast waits for the station's place. The station's live reading is asked for first, before its history (`Ecowitt.first_live`), so the dashboard's tiles and the first "weather now" are fresh within about a second of the port opening (Ecowitt's one request a second allowing), and a live reading being asked for is awaited by whoever else wants it rather than asked again (`Ecowitt._fetch_live`: a question waits only on one asked for a question, never on a background one queued behind the refreshes); the air sensor's reading, Home Assistant's entities of it, the cloud's name for it and today's gaps are its warm-up's (`AirGradient.settle`), and its first `warm()` keeps the reading just read (under 30 seconds old) instead of asking for it again. Home Assistant's `/api/states` answer is shared by everyone asking within 5 seconds (the station's and the air sensor's start ask at about the same moment). The public holiday calendar (`lib/public_holidays.py`) is built at the first holiday question, not at start: the station's start only checks the `holidays` package is installed and the time zone has a state. Every HTTP client shares one TLS context (`lib/tls.py`), made once, rather than each loading the certificates itself. The Docker image holds the bytecode of what the service imports at start and at its first requests (the Dockerfile's build step imports them), as its user can't write any.

## The pollen and forecast sources
Both are off by default. They are websites, not APIs (the pollen page is parsed), so they are fetched gently: only between 6 am and 6 pm local time, pollen every 30 minutes with a conditional request (a 304 costs the site almost nothing) and the forecast every 15 minutes (a failure is logged), and questions use the last fetch. The forecast's rain figure is the Bureau-style "chance of at least 1 mm": the share of Open-Meteo's ECMWF ensemble members (51) that put 1 mm or more on the day, to the nearest 5% (a second request to `ensemble-api.open-meteo.com`; if it is down, Open-Meteo's own figure, the chance of more than 0.1 mm in the wettest hour, is shown as "chance of rain"). The day's rain in the line reads `~17 mm (9–25 likely; ≥5 at 90%)`: the figure is Open-Meteo's own `precipitation_sum`; the range is the ensemble members' 10th to 90th percentile (`SPREAD` in `lib/forecast/source.py`) of the day's total, shown when wider than the total's rounding; the floor is the 10th percentile rounded down to a step of `FLOOR_LADDER` (2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 75, 100 mm), so `≥5 at 90%` means 90% of the runs give at least 5 mm (the percentage is 1 minus that quantile). A floor is never given when it reaches the rounded total. A day without one (showers, drizzle) reads the chance of at least 1 mm in its place (`~3 mm (1–3.5 likely; ≥1 at 80%)`), and a day with no ensemble has no range or chance (`~12 mm`, or `N% chance of rain` without a total). The report never fetches them: it always uses the cache. When a forecast day is later revised substantially (rain to dry or dry to rain, or the highest temperature more than 2 degrees different), a forecast alert is written, once per revision (`lib/alerts/forecast.py`). What they returned is kept in `conditions_cache.sqlite` (one row per distinct result), so a restart, even at night, starts from the last fetch. One fetch is made just after the start (ecobot-mcp's warm-up, or the first question) when nothing is cached, even at night; a restart in the day fetches then only if the last fetch is due again. `python scripts/conditions.py --lat .. --lon ..` fetches both once and prints what the report would show: run it before switching them on. Grass pollen and the thunderstorm asthma forecast both run October to December, so the pollen source only fetches, shows (in the report or an answer) and alerts in those months; outside them it makes no requests (not even at start), the report has no Pollen & asthma block, and a pollen question is told they only run October to December.

## How it stays fast and cheap

- Every line of a chart ends with its latest value beside it (zero included); the values of lines that end close together are spread apart so that none overlaps another, with a thin leader to the dot of one moved. A chart with several named lines has its colour key above the plot, in as few rows as fit. A chart is drawn when a client asks for it (as data, a PNG or a link), and an alert's chart is the same call run again with the latest readings (`lib/alerts/pictures.py`, `CHARTS`).
- Recent readings are refreshed every minute (the weather station: written to its storage, those still settling as provisional readings the next fetch replaces) and every 4 minutes (the air sensor, in memory), by ecobot-mcp. A question never waits for a refresh in flight when the storage already holds what it needs (the newest readings for 5 minutes after they were fetched), and its own Ecowitt requests go before the background ones. When it does wait for a fetch in flight (one fetch at a time per station and resolution, `Recent.turn` in `lib/ecowitt/fetch.py`), that fetch is hurried: its request still queued moves to the urgent line, and so do its later ones and those of a background fetch taking the resolution while the question waits. The keep-warm asks for the 5-minute and 30-minute tails before the 4-hour and daily ones, so a question on today, just after a start, waits for one request less.
- The whole history of both sensors, and the last pollen page and forecast, are kept in one SQLite file, `ecobot.sqlite` (`lib/storage`,
  docs/storage-plan.md). A background archive copies every Ecowitt cycle (5-minute, 30-minute, 4-hour, daily) before Ecowitt expires it, and backfills
  AirGradient day by day to where the sensor's data starts. Questions only go to the APIs for data the file is missing. Each sensor's
  local days are worked out as its readings are written (the air sensor's with its glitches taken out), so `weather_days`, `air_days`,
  the dashboard and the long charts read a row a day.
- A chart of 5-minute readings is shaded between each five minutes' lowest and highest, as Home Assistant's history graph is. Ecowitt's own 5-minute history has one number per reading and no range, so ecobot-mcp keeps the ranges itself, as the bands of the 5-minute readings in `ecobot.sqlite` (`Ecowitt.live_marks`): every live reading it takes (about one a minute, Ecowitt's real_time) is counted in the five minutes around the nearest 5-minute mark, and a stretch with two or more readings has a range, which widens that 5-minute reading's band. Temperature, humidity, dew point, feels like, pressure, solar, UV and the indoor readings are kept; wind has its gusts as its range and rain is bars. Every weather line is shaded, whatever the period (the old rule that left the lines of 31 days or fewer clean is gone). The ranges are promoted into wider buckets as a chart needs them: a 5-minute reading takes its own five minutes', a 30-minute reading (a week's or a month's chart) takes the lowest and highest of the half hour (its band widened from the 5-minute readings inside it whenever they are written, so a chart reads a few hundred rows a reading; it only ever widens Ecowitt's own range, or the lowest and highest of the 5-minute values), and a wider bucket the extremes of the 30-minute ones it holds. Only where ecobot-mcp was watching: before that the band is Ecowitt's own range, as ever, and past 31 days it is Ecowitt's daily record. The gauges' Daily and Monthly highs and lows take in the recorded ranges too (the year's row takes in the month's). Only a chart asks for them, at a cost of a few milliseconds a reading. About a reading a minute is as fine as Ecowitt's cloud gives, so a range is made of a handful of readings, fewer than Home Assistant's local feed.
- With the station's readings coming from the cloud and Home Assistant set (`MCP_HOMEASSISTANT_URL`, `MCP_HOMEASSISTANT_TOKEN`), the Ecowitt ranges come from it (`lib/ecowitt/homeassistant.py`); with Home Assistant as the station's source its history carries the bands with the readings, so nothing is read back. Its Ecowitt integration is sent every reading the gateway makes (every 16 to 60 seconds) and keeps each change for 10 days by default, which is finer than the cloud and covers the time ecobot-mcp was down. ecobot-mcp finds the station's entities by name (`sensor.<station>_outdoor_temperature` and so on), reads their history over the REST API (`/api/history/period`, at start for the last 31 days at most and then the last 3 hours every 15 minutes) and folds it into the same 5-minute ranges: a stretch's range is the values it changed to in that time and the one it held as it began. Home Assistant only records a change of state, so a stretch where nothing changed has a range of nothing, which counts. Temperature, humidity, dew point, feels like, VPD, pressure, solar and UV, and the indoor readings, are read. If Home Assistant can't be reached it is logged once and ecobot-mcp's own readings carry on.
- The air sensor has three sources (`MCP_AIRGRADIENT_SOURCE`; `lib/airgradient/source.py`, docs/storage-plan.md 3.12), each filling only what the ones before it lack, which the file's write rules do (a reading from ecobot-mcp itself ranks above Home Assistant's, Home Assistant's above the cloud's; a lower one fills only a cell a higher one left empty). The sensor's own server (`MCP_AIRGRADIENT_LOCAL_URL`) is read every minute: each 5 minutes' readings are a row (their mean, with their lowest and highest as its band, loaded as a reading's `_range` and shaded on the charts), and each reading is kept as a minute (`1min`, 10 days), so a chart of up to 8 hours is drawn a reading a minute, as the station's are. Home Assistant (`lib/airgradient/homeassistant.py`), when it has the sensor (its entities are found by name: `sensor.<device>_voc_index`, `_pm2_5`, `_carbon_dioxide` ...; with several sensors, the one whose reading is nearest), is read the same way while the sensor does not answer ecobot-mcp (its states, every minute: the live reading, and the rows and minutes), and its history (`/api/history/period`: 31 days at start, as far back as it has them, then the last 3 hours every 15 minutes) is read back for the times ecobot-mcp did not read the sensor at all: each 5 minutes' mean with its lowest and highest as the band, the bands of the buckets ecobot-mcp has, and the minutes of the last 9 hours. The cloud fills what neither has: the history before Home Assistant's 10 days (the backfill), the times both were down, and the sensor's name. Its current reading is the live one only when neither answers.
- Each metric of a lower source is checked against what ecobot-mcp holds from the ones above it before it fills a gap, over the five minutes both hold (within 2 µg/m³ or 15%, in four in five of them, is the same reading). The sensor corrects its PM2.5 as it is set to (the dashboard's correction when it takes its configuration from there, else its own, none by default) and Home Assistant shows that; the cloud gives the dashboard's. So a metric that differs (the raw PM2.5 against the corrected one) fills no gaps of the sources above it (they are drawn as gaps), and is said once in the log with the fix: set the sensor's PM2.5 correction to the dashboard's, or have it take its configuration from the dashboard. The cloud is asked for the whole of each finished day ecobot-mcp read itself (one request): the day's check, and its readings fill what ecobot-mcp lacked. With the sensor alone, nothing to check against, the start-up line says which correction it applies (from its `/config`).

## Days and totals

The `days` tool counts and ranks days by any reading (a day's highest or lowest humidity, pressure, UV, solar, wind, dew point and so on), from the stored day rows, so "how many days was UV 9 or more" is one cheap call (`count_only` returns just the counts; `group_by` month or year adds a count for each and draws them as a bar chart, a number above every bar; `stat` and `of` give a total, average, highest or lowest of any reading per month or year the same way, e.g. rain per month or the hottest day each year). A rain chart reaching back past the 30-minute readings (about a year) uses the daily records for the older days, and one asked for by month or year is monthly or yearly bars. The air sensor's metrics are counted the same way (days PM2.5 passed 25, the worst day each month), from the stored readings.

## Home Assistant as the weather station's source

- `MCP_ECOWITT_SOURCE` picks where the readings come from (`auto` is Home Assistant if it is set, the cloud filling in if its keys are set too; `MCP_AIRGRADIENT_SOURCE` does the same for the air sensor, see above). `lib/ecowitt/hass_api.py` answers the three calls ecobot-mcp makes of Ecowitt's cloud (`devices`, `realtime`, `history`) from Home Assistant, in the cloud's shapes and units, so the cache, archive, charts and alerts are the same code whichever answers. `lib/ecowitt/hass_map.py` is the table of entities (`sensor.<station>_<reading>`, found by name) and `hass_history.py` tells Home Assistant's recorded changes as the cloud's buckets (1 minute, 5, 30, 4 hours, a day), time-weighted means, the highest gust, the last rain counter, with the true lowest and highest of each bucket as `_low`/`_high`.
- Both set: `lib/ecowitt/fallback.py`. Home Assistant answers; a live reading older than 10 minutes, or none, is replaced by the cloud's if newer; the buckets Home Assistant has none of (before it began recording, while it was down) are asked of the cloud in one request for the span they cover. A failure of either is logged once, with its recovery.
- Faster: the live reading is polled every 30 seconds (`Ecowitt.watch_live`), a reading newer than the last is kept for "weather now", the report and the gauges, and the rain alert looks at it at once (`WeatherMonitor.check_rain`) instead of at the next minute's refresh. The gateway itself reports about every minute, so the alert is within 30 seconds of that. A chart of up to 8 hours is drawn a reading a minute (`1min`, Home Assistant only). ecobot-mcp takes the minutes as they come (the last 9 hours at start, then at each new live reading) into the storage's `1min` rows (`lib/ecowitt/query.py` holds the constants) and drops what is older, so such a chart is drawn without asking Home Assistant; if the minutes don't reach back to its start it uses the 5-minute readings, as longer charts do.
- Caveats: Home Assistant keeps 10 days of states unless its recorder is set otherwise, so older history comes from the cache ecobot-mcp built, or from the cloud where it is set. An answer with nothing in it from Home Assistant alone is not recorded as fetched.
