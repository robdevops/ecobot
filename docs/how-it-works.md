# How it works

For people changing the code. Tests and the GitHub checks are in [`DEVELOPMENT.md`](../DEVELOPMENT.md).

## Docker logging

The bot logs DEBUG and INFO lines to stdout and WARN and ERROR lines to stderr (`lib/logs.py`), so Docker, and its journald driver, can tell them apart; under systemd, where both go to the journal, each line starts with its syslog priority (`<4>` ...), which the journal takes as its level and strips, so `journalctl` colours warnings and errors. `compose.yaml` gives the container Docker's `local` log driver, which compresses and rotates its files (5 files of 10 MB). Read the log with `docker logs -tf ecobot` (`-t` for the timestamps Docker records).

## The pollen and forecast sources
Both are off by default. They are websites, not APIs (the pollen page is parsed), so they are fetched gently: only between 6 am and 6 pm local time, pollen every 30 minutes with a conditional request (a 304 costs the site almost nothing) and the forecast every 15 minutes (a failure is logged), and questions use the last fetch. The forecast's rain figure is the Bureau-style "chance of at least 1 mm": the share of Open-Meteo's ECMWF ensemble members (51) that put 1 mm or more on the day, to the nearest 5% (a second request to `ensemble-api.open-meteo.com`; if it is down, Open-Meteo's own figure, the chance of more than 0.1 mm in the wettest hour, is shown as "chance of rain"). The report never fetches them: it always uses the cache. When a forecast day that was sent to a chat (in a report or an answer) is later revised substantially (rain to dry or dry to rain, or the highest temperature more than 2 degrees different), that chat is told, once per revision (`lib/alerts/forecast.py`). What they returned is kept in `conditions_cache.sqlite` (one row per distinct result), so a restart, even at night, starts from the last fetch. One fetch is made at start (or at the first question) when nothing is cached, even at night. `python scripts/conditions.py --lat .. --lon ..` fetches both once and prints what the report would show: run it before switching them on. Grass pollen and the thunderstorm asthma forecast both run October to December, so the pollen source only fetches, shows (in the report or an answer) and alerts in those months; outside them it makes no requests (not even at start), the report has no Pollen & asthma block, and a pollen question is told they only run October to December.

## How it stays fast and cheap

- Private chats also get a persistent button keyboard (`lib/templates.py`): tapping one sends its label as a message (a 3x3 grid: Help & Alerts = what the bot can do, then the alert settings, Status, Rain; Weather = every reading, a panel each, Temperature, Humidity; Air Quality = every air metric, Particulates = PM1, PM2.5 and PM10 on one chart, Wind, which the Irrigation button replaces when the controller is configured; every chart button is the last day). `/keyboard` shows it, `/keyboard off` hides it. When the buttons change, the bot says "Buttons updated." with the new keyboard to each private chat at its next start, and any chat missed gets it with its next reply (the bot remembers which version each chat has; a chat that hid it keeps it hidden). Typed "weather now" shows every reading the station has. Weather lines of 31 days or fewer are drawn with no shaded min–max range (except wind's gusts; AirGradient charts keep theirs); longer periods are daily with the range. Every line ends with its latest value beside it (zero included). A single-panel chart's caption is one line ("Temperature, since Tue 7 Jul 2026" when the period runs up to today, else "Temperature, Wed 24 – Tue 30 Sep 2026"); a chart of several panels has its title and then its time range on a second line; one chart and a short answer go as one message (the answer under the chart's title in its caption), a longer answer is a message of its own sent just before the chart. A single chart carries period buttons under it (1D, 7D, 1M, 3M and 1Y, the chart's own marked with ●; pressing it does nothing): pressing one redraws that chart in place for the new period (`lib/periods.py`, `Bot.on_period_button`). The bot keeps the question behind its last 300 charts in its state file, so the buttons keep working across restarts; an older chart answers "out of date, please ask again". A reply with period buttons doesn't carry the persistent keyboard; that follows on the next reply. A group of several charts has no buttons. Charts drawn in the last 10 minutes are kept (the newest 60), so toggling between periods brings a chart back instantly without a new question. Any reading or "weather" with a period of two days or more ("humidity 7d", "weather 30d", "particulates 7d") is a chart, sent with no text.
- In private chats the bot shows Telegram's "Thinking..." draft (sendMessageDraft, re-sent every 20 s, as drafts expire after 30) and streams the answer into it before sending the real message; groups show "typing...". A "message generation stopped" update is only logged.
- The model only reasons for predictions ("will it rain?") and "describe it" questions, or when asked to think, try, reason, predict, estimate, grind or whirl.
- Simple highs/lows, chart and "air quality now" questions are fetched by the bot first, so the
  model is called once, at the end, just to word the answer.
- Recent readings are kept in memory, refreshed every minute (the weather station) and every 4 minutes (the air sensor); a question that may need them starts a refresh as it arrives (a chart made in code doesn't: it reads just what it needs). A question never waits for a refresh in flight when the cache and memory already hold what it needs, and its own Ecowitt requests go before the background ones.
- A chart is drawn when it is asked for (a question, a button, an alert), and uploaded once: sending it again (to the next chat an alert goes to, toggling back to a period drawn lately, the next press of a button) uses Telegram's copy. With `TELEGRAM_PRELOAD_CHAT` the other periods of a chart are uploaded ahead of the press.
- Each chart reply logs (at DEBUG) where its time went: `Chart ms: data 12, calc 6, draw 112, PNG 41 (22 KB), send 812`, or `Chart ms: drawn 40s ago, send 210 (Telegram's copy)` for one drawn lately.
- The whole history of both sensors is kept in SQLite (`ecowitt_cache.sqlite`,
  `airgradient_cache.sqlite`): a background archive copies every Ecowitt cycle (5-minute, 30-minute,
  4-hour, daily) before Ecowitt expires it, and backfills AirGradient day by day to where the sensor's
  data starts. Questions only go to the APIs for data the cache is missing.
- A chart of 5-minute readings is shaded between each five minutes' lowest and highest, as Home Assistant's history graph is. Ecowitt's own 5-minute history has one number per reading and no range, so the bot keeps the ranges itself (`ecowitt_live.sqlite`; `lib/ecowitt/live.py`): every live reading it takes (about one a minute, Ecowitt's real_time) is folded into the five minutes around the nearest 5-minute mark, and a stretch with two or more readings has a range. Temperature, humidity, dew point, feels like, pressure, solar, UV and the indoor readings are kept; wind has its gusts as its range and rain is bars. Every weather line is shaded, whatever the period (the old rule that left the lines of 31 days or fewer clean is gone). The ranges are promoted into wider buckets as a chart needs them: a 5-minute reading takes its own five minutes', a 30-minute reading (a week's or a month's chart) takes the lowest and highest of the half hour (kept rolled up as the ranges come in, `slots` in `ecowitt_live.sqlite`, so a chart reads a few hundred rows a reading; it only ever widens Ecowitt's own range, or the lowest and highest of the 5-minute values), and a wider bucket the extremes of the 30-minute ones it holds. Only where the bot was watching: before that the band is Ecowitt's own range, as ever, and past 31 days it is Ecowitt's daily record. The gauges' Daily and Monthly highs and lows take in the recorded ranges too (the year's row takes in the month's). Only a chart asks for them, at a cost of a few milliseconds a reading. About a reading a minute is as fine as Ecowitt's cloud gives, so a range is made of a handful of readings, fewer than Home Assistant's local feed.
- With Home Assistant set (`HOMEASSISTANT_URL`, `HOMEASSISTANT_TOKEN`), the Ecowitt ranges also come from it (`lib/ecowitt/homeassistant.py`). Its Ecowitt integration is sent every reading the gateway makes (every 16 to 60 seconds) and keeps each change for 10 days by default, which is finer than the cloud and covers the time the bot was down. The bot finds the station's entities by name (`sensor.<station>_outdoor_temperature` and so on), reads their history over the REST API (`/api/history/period`, at start for the last 31 days at most and then the last 3 hours every 15 minutes) and folds it into the same 5-minute ranges: a stretch's range is the values it changed to in that time and the one it held as it began. Home Assistant only records a change of state, so a stretch where nothing changed has a range of nothing, which counts. Temperature, humidity, dew point, feels like, VPD, pressure, solar and UV, and the indoor readings, are read. If Home Assistant can't be reached it is logged once and the bot's own readings carry on.
- The air sensor's ranges come from it the same way (`lib/airgradient/homeassistant.py`): AirGradient's cloud keeps one average per 5 minutes and the sensor's own server only the reading of the moment, so the bot has a range only for the time it read the sensor itself. Home Assistant's AirGradient integration reads it every minute; the bot finds its entities by name (`sensor.<device>_voc_index`, `_pm2_5`, `_carbon_dioxide` ...; with several sensors, the one whose reading is nearest) and keeps the lowest and highest of each 5 minutes where Home Assistant's average for them agrees with the reading the bot holds (an entity that shows another variant of a reading, such as a raw PM2.5 beside the corrected one, disagrees and is left out, said once in the log). The bot's own ranges are never replaced. Same schedule as the station's: 31 days at start, then the last 3 hours every 15 minutes.
- AirGradient is the same: its cloud gives one value per 5 minutes (no range, and no finer bucket: asking for one changes nothing). With the sensor's own server (`AIRGRADIENT_LOCAL_URL`) the bot reads it every minute and averages each 5 minutes; it now also keeps each metric's lowest and highest reading in that bucket (the `spreads` table of `airgradient_cache.sqlite`, loaded as a reading's `_range`), and its charts shade between them. Only buckets the bot recorded itself have a range, and only the last 31 days of them are kept (`lines.RANGE_KEEP_DAYS`, for Ecowitt's too: the 1M chart's 30 days and a day over); the cloud's, and everything from before the local sensor was switched on, are single values.

## The report and plain lookups
"report" (and "status", "sitrep") and "weather now" are written in code, with no model call (`lib/report.py`): the bot fetches the tools' results together and lays them out, so they are quick and always the same. Sun, UV and wind are left out when zero (night, calm). The report is: Weather station, Air quality, and Pollen & asthma and Forecast when those sources are on (pollen and forecast always come from the cache). The report carries a 🔄 Refresh button that reads everything again and redraws it in the same message (`lib/report_menu.py`). With the weather station, the report is the caption of a picture of its temperature, humidity and rain gauges (`ecowitt.dashboard.STATUS`, drawn by `lib/gauges.py` while the report is fetched); Refresh redraws both. The gauges' highs and lows for the days before yesterday are read from the cache once a day and kept; yesterday's and today's are read each time. A report longer than a caption (1,024 characters) goes after the picture as a message of its own; a refreshed one that has grown too long keeps its first sections.

A plain chart request ("rain chart 7d", "plot temperature and humidity", "air quality 30d", the chart buttons) is also drawn and written up in code, with no model call (the chart goes out with just its title as the caption; the write-up is kept in the chat's memory, not sent) (`intent.weather_chart`, `report.chart_caption`): the period, then low and high per reading (the average first if one was asked), the peak and rating for air quality, and for a rain chart the least and most rain and whether more is expected. Anything that asks for correlation, analysis, a forecast, thinking or a description, or compares readings or names a particular date or time, still goes to the model.

Other plain lookups are answered in code too, from the one tool result (`intent.plain_lookup`, `report.lookup`): a single reading now ("how hot is it", "is it raining", "uv"), the air ("how's the air", "pm10"), pollen, the plain forecast ("forecast", "7 day forecast"), the highs, lows or average of a short period ("hottest today", "average temp yesterday", "highest humidity yesterday") and what the bot can do ("what can you do"). Any question with forecast, correlation, thinking, analysis or description words, a comparison, or anything beyond the reading itself goes to the model; so does a lookup whose result can't be used.

`weather_days` can count and rank days by any reading (a day's highest or lowest humidity, pressure, UV, solar, wind, dew point and so on), from the cache, so "how many days was UV 9 or more" is one cheap call (`count_only` returns just the counts; `group_by` month or year adds a count for each and draws them as a bar chart, a number above every bar; `stat` and `of` give a total, average, highest or lowest of any reading per month or year the same way, e.g. rain per month or the hottest day each year). A plain "rain by month for 2 years" or "average temperature per year" (one reading, a figure for each month or year) is worked out in code the same way (`weather_days`/`air_days` with `stat`, `of`, `group_by`): one bar for each month or year, a one-line caption with the figure for the whole period. A rain chart reaching back past the 30-minute readings (about a year) uses the daily records for the older days, and one asked for by month or year is monthly or yearly bars, even if the model answers it. A plain "days over UVI 10 by year" or "count days over PM2.5 of 90 per year" (one reading against one limit, optionally per year or month, over the whole record unless a period is named) is worked out in code without the model, so it always gives the same bar chart; if the model is asked instead and leaves out the by-year or by-month grouping the words ask for, it is added. `air_days` does the same for the air sensor's metrics (days PM2.5 passed 25, the worst day each month), from the stored readings. A question that pulls in a lot of data is not refused: when the tool results of one question pass twice what a 500-point query returns (about 10,000 tokens of input, on every later step too), the bot sends a one-line heads-up to the chat before the model is sent them, then carries on without waiting for a reply (`lib/timeutil.py` `WARN_TOKENS`).

What still goes to the model is kept small (and, for "will it rain?" and a described today or yesterday, the readings it would fetch first are fetched ahead of it, so it answers in one call; a chat's older answers are cut to their first lines, the last one kept whole; the `weather_days`/`air_days` definitions list their fields once): the system prompt has a core that is the same on every call (so the provider can cache it) and adds guidance only for the topics a question touches (`intent.topics`: air, days, correlation, outlook, wind, describing a day, the bot itself); every call sends the same tool list in the same order (so the provider can cache the tools and the core prompt together; a changing list cost cache hits); a chat keeps its last 16 messages; `weather_history` leaves out humidity when only temperature was named (and the reverse) and, for periods over a week, sends only the days of the records unless day by day was asked for. Looking ahead, analysis and describing a day think a little (low), correlation and "think" more (medium), everything else not at all.

The system prompt starts with what never changes (the model provider caches the start of a prompt) and ends with the date ranges and the time.

## Home Assistant as the weather station's source

- `ECOWITT_SOURCE` picks where the readings come from (`auto` is Home Assistant if it is set, the cloud filling in if its keys are set too). `lib/ecowitt/hass_api.py` answers the three calls the bot makes of Ecowitt's cloud (`devices`, `realtime`, `history`) from Home Assistant, in the cloud's shapes and units, so the cache, archive, charts and alerts are the same code whichever answers. `lib/ecowitt/hass_map.py` is the table of entities (`sensor.<station>_<reading>`, found by name) and `hass_history.py` tells Home Assistant's recorded changes as the cloud's buckets (1 minute, 5, 30, 4 hours, a day), time-weighted means, the highest gust, the last rain counter, with the true lowest and highest of each bucket as `_low`/`_high`.
- Both set: `lib/ecowitt/fallback.py`. Home Assistant answers; a live reading older than 10 minutes, or none, is replaced by the cloud's if newer; the buckets Home Assistant has none of (before it began recording, while it was down) are asked of the cloud in one request for the span they cover. A failure of either is logged once, with its recovery.
- Faster: the live reading is polled every 5 seconds (`Ecowitt.watch_live`), a reading newer than the last is kept for "weather now", the report and the gauges, and the rain alert looks at it at once (`WeatherMonitor.check_rain`) instead of at the next minute's refresh. The gateway itself reports about every minute, so the alert is within seconds of that. A chart of up to 8 hours is drawn a reading a minute (`1min`, Home Assistant only). The bot takes the minutes as they come (the last 9 hours at start, then at each new live reading) into `ecowitt_minutes.sqlite` (`lib/ecowitt/minutes.py`) and drops what is older, so such a chart is drawn without asking Home Assistant; if the minutes don't reach back to its start it uses the 5-minute readings, as longer charts do.
- Caveats: Home Assistant keeps 10 days of states unless its recorder is set otherwise, so older history comes from the cache the bot built, or from the cloud where it is set. An answer with nothing in it from Home Assistant alone is not recorded as fetched.
